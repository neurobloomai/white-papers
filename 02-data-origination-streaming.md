## Abstract

WP-01 defined the AI Inference Streaming primitive: fund compute infrastructure, collect a perpetual toll on inference output. This paper extends the same structure one layer deeper — to **data origination**.

The most defensible position in AI is not the model. It is the data the model cannot be trained without. In several high-value domains, that data is being generated by small, capital-constrained companies with genuine, irreplaceable technological moats. These companies are not failing because their science is weak. They are failing because capital markets cannot yet see what the data is worth to an AI system that needs it.

We propose **Data Origination Streaming (DOS)**: a structure in which AI labs or purpose-built intermediaries fund the survival and scaling of domain-specific data originators — in exchange for a perpetual, low-cost stream of the data they produce. The funder gets the data asset. The originator gets the runway. Neither gives up equity in the other.

---

## 1. The Data Problem at the Frontier

General-purpose AI models have largely exhausted publicly available text. The next capability frontier is domain-specific reasoning — models that operate with expert-level accuracy in medicine, materials, climate, and biological systems.

These models require data that does not exist on the internet:

```
  Domain                  Data type                    Characteristic
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Genomics                Structural variant maps      Instrument-generated
  Drug discovery          Interaction networks         Wet lab originated
  Materials science       Novel compound properties    Synthesis-derived
  Climate systems         Sub-grid sensor readings     Physical network
  Surgical systems        Annotated procedure data     OR-embedded
```

Each of these data types shares three properties:

1. **Irreplaceable** — cannot be synthesized, scraped, or approximated
2. **Accumulating** — more valuable as the dataset scales over time
3. **Controlled** — generated by specialized instruments owned by a small number of companies

The companies that own these instruments are frequently small, pre-profitability, and structurally mispriced by capital markets — which evaluate them on revenue multiples rather than on the strategic value of the data asset they are quietly building.

---

## 2. The Structural Mismatch

Capital markets see a small instrumentation company and price it on current revenue. An AI lab that needs what that company generates should see something entirely different — a data originator whose output becomes increasingly irreplaceable as domain-specific AI develops.

This is the same mismatch the streaming model exploited in mining. Base metal operators sold precious metal byproduct streams cheaply because those metals were secondary to their core business. The streamer valued what others treated as noise.

Here, domain-specific data is being generated as a byproduct of running instruments, conducting trials, or operating sensor networks. It is priced near zero by the companies generating it. Its value to a foundation model is orders of magnitude higher.

---

## 3. The Data Origination Streaming Structure

**Data Origination Streaming Agreement (DOSA):**

> A capital provider funds a domain-specific data originator's operations or instrument deployment. In return, the funder receives a contractual right to access a defined percentage of all data generated — **in perpetuity** — at a fixed, near-zero cost agreed at deal inception.

```
  At deal time:
    Capital provider →  funds originator's scaling
    Originator       →  perpetual data stream at fixed cost

  Over time:
    Instruments deployed  →  data accumulates
    Funder cost           →  fixed, near-zero
    Strategic value       →  compounds with dataset scale

  At maturity:
    Funder holds the only large-scale dataset
    in the domain — training substrate for a
    foundation model no competitor can replicate
```

The funder's asset appreciates as the dataset scales. The cost does not. This is the streaming spread applied to data.

---

## 4. Domain Illustrations

The structure generalizes across any domain where irreplaceable data is being generated by capital-constrained originators:

**Genomics and structural biology**  
Certain sequencing and imaging modalities capture biological structure at resolutions that complementary technologies cannot. The data they produce is not redundant with existing genomic databases — it fills gaps that are structurally invisible to lower-resolution instruments. A foundation model for rare disease diagnosis or structural variant interpretation requires this layer specifically.

**Materials discovery**  
Novel compound synthesis generates experimental property data that no simulation can replace. Each synthesis run produces a data point that exists nowhere else. At scale, this becomes the empirical ground truth for materials AI — the difference between a model that reasons about known materials and one that can discover new ones.

**Climate and environmental sensing**  
Sub-grid sensor networks generate microclimate data at resolutions that satellite or weather station data cannot approximate. For foundation models that reason about agricultural yield, infrastructure resilience, or insurance risk, this resolution is not a refinement — it is a categorical difference in what the model can know.

**Surgical and procedural systems**  
Embedded procedural systems generate annotated footage and outcome data that cannot be reconstructed from existing medical literature. A surgical AI foundation model requires this origination layer. It cannot be approximated from text.

## 5. Why AI Labs Are the Natural Seat Holders

Three properties make frontier AI labs the natural DOSA counterparty:

**5.1 Asymmetric valuation**  
Capital markets price data originators on revenue. AI labs price them on training data scarcity. Only one of these valuations is correct for the next decade. Labs have the information advantage that the streaming model requires.

**5.2 Mission alignment**  
For a lab whose mission includes building capable domain-specific AI, funding the data substrate is not a distraction — it is the mission expressed at the infrastructure layer. The DOSA is a capital deployment mechanism that directly serves model capability.

**5.3 Compounding competitive moat**  
A DOSA creates a structural barrier. The funder holds a perpetual stream of domain data that no competitor can access without either replicating the instrument network — which takes years and significant capital — or licensing from the funder. The data becomes a moat not just for the originator but for the holder.

---

## 6. The Coordination Layer

As with AI Inference Streaming, the DOSA requires a trusted coordination layer for:

- **Provenance** — verifiable chain from instrument to dataset
- **Attribution** — which data flows under which agreement
- **Integrity** — tamper-proof origination records
- **Versioning** — how agreements adapt as instrument generations evolve

These requirements map onto the same primitives needed for multi-agent coordination: trusted state, verifiable handoff, relational accounting. The infrastructure that enables intent fidelity across agent networks is structurally suited to serve as the accounting substrate for data royalty agreements.

The financial primitive and the coordination infrastructure are not separate problems — they are the same problem expressed at different layers.

---

## 7. The Portfolio Approach

No single DOSA is sufficient. The same diversification logic that makes a streaming fund stronger than any single mine applies here:

```
  A portfolio of DOSAs across domains:
    Genomics, materials, climate, surgical, environmental

  Each agreement: perpetual data royalty, no equity exchange
  Capital deployed: concentrated where data scarcity is highest
  Return profile:  strategic data asset accumulation,
                   not financial yield

  The portfolio holder owns the irreplaceable data substrate
  for the next generation of domain-specific foundation models
```

This portfolio does not exist as a structured instrument. It is the institutional white space that follows AI inference streaming.

---

## 8. Risks

**Exclusivity vs. openness**  
Exclusive data access may conflict with open science norms in certain domains. Structures that grant priority access or preferential pricing, rather than full exclusivity, are likely more viable in regulated fields.

**Originator survival risk**  
If the originator fails before the dataset reaches critical mass, the funder holds an incomplete asset. Milestone-based capital tranches reduce this exposure.

**Data quality**  
The value of a data stream depends on annotation, curation, and standardization. The funder must account for the curation layer, not just the origination layer.

**Modality obsolescence**  
Data modalities can be superseded by new technologies. Agreements should be structured at the originator level, not the specific instrument generation, to capture future instrument improvements under the same terms.

---

## 9. Conclusion

The most defensible position in AI is not the model. It is the data the model cannot be trained without — irreplaceable, accumulating, and currently being generated by companies the capital markets have largely overlooked.

Data Origination Streaming is the instrument that resolves the mismatch:

```
  Originator:   gets survival capital, no equity dilution
  Funder:       gets perpetual data stream at fixed cost
  Domain:       gets scaled instrumentation it couldn't finance alone
  Foundation model: gets training data no competitor can replicate
```

The streaming model taught us that the toll collector beats the operator. In AI, the most valuable toll is not on inference. It is on the irreplaceable data that makes inference worth anything at all.

The instrument is undefined. The assets are real. The seat is empty.
