## Abstract

WP-01 defined the AI Inference Streaming primitive: fund compute infrastructure, collect a perpetual toll on inference output. This paper extends the same structure one layer deeper — to **data origination**.

The most defensible position in AI is not the model. It is the data the model cannot be trained without. In several high-value domains, that data is being generated by small, capital-starved companies with genuine, irreplaceable technological moats. These companies are not failing because their science is weak. They are failing because the capital markets cannot yet see what the data is worth to an AI lab.

We propose **Data Origination Streaming (DOS)**: a structure in which AI labs or purpose-built intermediaries fund the survival and scaling of domain-specific data originators — in exchange for a perpetual, low-cost stream of the data they produce. The funder gets the data asset. The originator gets the runway. Neither gives up equity in the other.

This is not philanthropy. It is the most rational capital allocation available to any AI lab that intends to build domain-specific foundation models.

## 1. The Data Problem at the Frontier

General-purpose AI models have largely exhausted the internet. The next capability frontier is domain-specific reasoning — models that can diagnose rare disease, discover drug targets, predict material properties, or reason about climate systems with expert-level accuracy.

These models require data that does not exist on the internet:

```
  Domain                  Data type                       Who generates it
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Rare genomics           Structural variant maps         OGM instruments
  Drug discovery          Protein interaction networks    Wet lab + sequencing
  Materials science       Novel compound properties       Synthesis + characterization
  Climate systems         Sub-grid sensor readings        Physical sensor networks
  Surgical robotics       Annotated procedure footage     OR-embedded systems
```

Each of these data types shares three properties:

1. **Irreplaceable** — cannot be synthesized, scraped, or approximated
2. **Accumulating** — more valuable as the dataset grows over time
3. **Controlled** — generated by a small number of specialized instruments or platforms, often owned by a single company

The companies that own these instruments are frequently small, pre-profitability, and structurally invisible to traditional capital markets — which price them on revenue multiples rather than on the strategic value of the data asset they are building.

The capital market sees:

```

    Revenue        $15M
    Growth          3%
    Op Margin     -118%
    Market Cap     $14M
    Verdict:        avoid / terminal risk
```

The AI lab should see:

```
  Same company:
    Generates structural variant maps
    that no other technology produces
    at any price

    Each instrument placed in a hospital
    generates irreplaceable patient-level
    genomic data for decades

    At scale: the only complete map of
    human structural variation in existence

    To a biological foundation model:
    worth orders of magnitude more than
    the company's market cap
```

This is the same mismatch WPM exploited in mining — base metal miners selling silver streams cheaply because silver was not their primary focus. Here, small biotech/instrumentation companies are generating AI-critical data as a byproduct of their core business, and pricing it accordingly: near zero.

## 3. The Data Origination Streaming Structure

**Data Origination Streaming Agreement (DOSA):**

> An AI lab or intermediary provides capital to a domain-specific data originator. In return, the funder receives a contractual right to access a defined percentage of all data generated by the originator's instruments or platforms — **in perpetuity** — at a fixed, near-zero cost agreed at deal inception.

```
  At deal time:
    AI lab       →  $20M to genomics company
    Company      →  perpetual access to X% of all
                    OGM data generated, forever,
                    at $Y per genome (fixed, near-zero)

  At year 1:
    100 instruments deployed
    10,000 genomes mapped
    AI lab cost: $Y × 10,000 (near-zero)
    Strategic value: training data for rare disease model
                     that no competitor can replicate

  At year 5:
    1,000 instruments deployed
    500,000 genomes mapped
    AI lab cost: still $Y per genome (fixed)
    Strategic value: the definitive human structural
                     variation dataset — moat for
                     any biological foundation model
```

The AI lab's asset appreciates as the dataset scales. The cost does not. This is the WPM spread applied to data.

## 4. Case Study: The Optical Genome Mapping Gap

Bionano Genomics (BNGO) produces optical genome mapping (OGM) instruments. The technology uses nanochannel arrays to stretch and image entire DNA molecules — capturing chromosomal rearrangements, large insertions and deletions, and copy number variations at a resolution that neither short-read (Illumina) nor long-read (PacBio) sequencing can match.

```
  Technology comparison:

  Illumina short-read   →  150bp reads, cheap, high throughput
                            misses: structural variants > 1kb

  PacBio long-read      →  20,000bp reads, expensive, accurate
                            misses: large chromosomal rearrangements

  Bionano OGM           →  whole chromosome imaging
                            catches: what both above miss
                            unique physics — not a faster/cheaper
                            version of existing technology
```

OGM is not competing with sequencing. It is completing it. The three technologies together produce a picture of the genome that none produces alone.

For a biological foundation model — one that reasons about rare disease, cancer genomics, or structural birth defects — the OGM layer is the one most absent from current training data. It is also the one most likely to be lost if BNGO does not survive.

**The DOSA applied here:**

```
  Funder provides:    $30-50M (survival + scaling capital)
  Bionano provides:   perpetual access to all OGM data
                      generated on funded instruments

  Bionano gets:       runway, instrument deployment capital,
                      a strategic partner not a creditor
  Funder gets:        the only large-scale human OGM dataset
                      in existence, growing indefinitely
                      at near-zero marginal cost
```

Neither party gives up equity. The instrument is a data royalty, not a financing agreement.

## 6. Why AI Labs Are the Natural Seat Holders

Three properties make frontier AI labs the natural DOSA counterparty:

**6.1 They know what the data is worth**

Capital markets price small instrumentation companies on revenue. AI labs price them on training data scarcity. Only one of these valuations is correct for the next decade. Labs have the asymmetric information advantage.

**6.2 They have the capital and the mission alignment**

For a lab whose stated mission is building the most capable and beneficial AI, funding the data substrate for biological, materials, or climate foundation models is not a distraction — it is the mission. The DOSA is a capital deployment mechanism that directly serves model capability.

**6.3 The data moat compounds against competitors**

A DOSA creates a structural barrier. If Anthropic holds a perpetual stream of all OGM data from Bionano instruments, no competitor can train an equivalent biological foundation model without either replicating the instrument network (years, billions) or licensing from Anthropic. The data becomes a moat not just for the originator — but for the funder.

## 7. The Coordination Layer

As with AI Inference Streaming (WP-01), the DOSA requires a trusted coordination layer for:

- **Attribution** — which data was generated under which agreement
- **Metering** — how much data has been streamed and at what cost
- **Integrity** — tamper-proof provenance from instrument to model training
- **Versioning** — as instrument generations improve, how does the agreement adapt

These requirements map directly onto the primitives being developed in multi-agent coordination protocols: trusted state, verifiable handoff, relational ledger. The same infrastructure that enables intent fidelity across agent networks can serve as the accounting substrate for data royalty agreements.

In this framing, coordination protocols are not just agent infrastructure. They are the legal and technical primitive for a new class of AI asset — the data stream.

## 8. Risks and Open Questions

**Exclusivity vs. openness**  
A DOSA that grants exclusive data access may conflict with open science norms in genomics and climate research. Structures that grant priority access or preferential pricing, rather than exclusivity, may be more viable in regulated domains.

**Regulatory classification**  
Is a DOSA a financial instrument, a data licensing agreement, or a research partnership? Classification varies by jurisdiction and affects tax treatment, securities regulation, and accounting.

**Data quality and standardization**  
Instrument-generated data is only as valuable as its annotation, curation, and standardization. The funder must either trust the originator's data quality or fund the curation layer separately.

**Originator survival risk**  
If the originator fails before the dataset reaches critical mass, the funder holds a stream of incomplete data. Milestone-based capital tranches, rather than upfront lump sums, reduce this exposure.

**Model deprecation analog**  
Just as AI models become obsolete, data modalities can be superseded. OGM may be complemented or partially replaced by future sequencing technologies. Agreements should cover the originator's instrument output broadly, not specific technology generations.

## 9. The Portfolio Approach

No single DOSA is sufficient. The same diversification logic that makes WPM stronger than any single mine applies here:

```
  A purpose-built Data Origination Streaming fund:

  10-15 domain-specific data originators
  across genomics, materials, climate, robotics

  Each DOSA structured as a perpetual data royalty
  Capital deployed: $200-500M across portfolio
  Return profile: not financial yield —
                  strategic data asset accumulation

  The fund holder owns the irreplaceable data
  substrate for the next generation of
  domain-specific foundation models
```

This fund does not exist. It is the next institutional white space after AI inference streaming.

## 10. Conclusion

The most defensible position in AI is not the model. It is the data the model cannot be trained without — data that is irreplaceable, accumulating, and currently being generated by companies the capital markets have written off.

Data Origination Streaming is the instrument that aligns the incentives:

```
  Small innovator:   gets survival capital, no equity dilution
  AI lab / funder:   gets perpetual data stream at fixed cost
  Domain:            gets scaled instrumentation it couldn't fund
  Foundation model:  gets the training data nobody else has
```

The WPM model taught us that in resource industries, the toll collector beats the operator. The same logic applies in AI — and the most valuable toll is not on inference. It is on the irreplaceable data that makes inference worth anything at all.

The instrument is undefined. The assets are real. The seat is empty.
